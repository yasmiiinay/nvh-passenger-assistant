# Data exploration — observations for the report

Generated by the project's data-exploration script (kept in the private development archive). Every statement below is a **dataset** observation
read from the frozen manifests; none is a model result. Model behaviour on these data is reported separately
(development runs and the independent Blind v3 evaluation).

## Visual data (`data/images/`, 101 images)
- Label imbalance: 60% of images are `out_of_scope` (61), leaving 40 in-scope images across 9 labels; `lounge` has 1 image, `information` and `medical` 2 each, `transport` 11.
- Two vision-prompt categories have **no images at all**: `accessibility`, `security`. Their recognition rests on CLIP's zero-shot text prompts alone and was never checked on the development split.
- Style bias: 64 of 101 images (63%) are clean AIGA/DOT vector icons rendered from Wikimedia Commons; photographed signage contributes 28 good-light and 9 degraded images, all author-supplied (`own_photo`) and mostly photographs of the same AIGA-style pictograms.
- Split: 37 development / 64 held-out. Development in-scope images are 19 in total, so the vision thresholds (τ_high, τ_low, margin) were set on very few positives per label.
- Documented hard cases exist but are few: 9 defocused, 5 angled, 4 colour-inverted, 7 non-sign photographs.
- Visually related symbols cross label boundaries by design (suitcase motifs in `check_in` and `baggage`; the customs officer icon is `out_of_scope` but nearest to `security`), which is the intended stress on similarity-based retrieval.

## Text data (`data/text/`)
- Intent exemplars are balanced by construction: 95 authored phrasings, 6–7 per intent over 15 intents, all in the development split; there is no held-out exemplar set, so nearest-exemplar intent prediction is only tested indirectly through the labelled query sets.
- Labelled queries are small: 43 seed (dev), 36 held-out, 3 spoken, 15 QA regression = 97 in total; `find_gate` (12) and `find_transport` (10) dominate, `find_restaurant` and `find_restroom` have 3 each.
- All queries were authored by the developer or the QA sessions; two `non_english` cases exist but the system is English-only. Real passenger phrasing, typos and code-switching are not represented.
- Identifier handling is deterministic and dominant: 9 entity types are extracted by regex and gazetteer before any embedding; the tokenisation example shows why (WordPiece splits identifiers into fragments).

## Speech data (`data/audio/`, 125 clips + 4 derived)
- 120 of 125 clips (96%) are synthetic macOS TTS voices (5 voices × 24 utterances); one human speaker contributed 5 clips. Accent, age, spontaneous phrasing and disfluency diversity are therefore minimal.
- Every manifest clip is recorded as `quiet` / `clean`; acoustic degradation exists only in the 4 derived clips (−52 dBFS gain, 5 dB SNR white noise). Background noise from a real terminal is not represented.
- Durations are short (0.87–4.67 s, mean 2.08 s, median 1.74 s); human clips average 3.89 s against 2.00 s for TTS. All files are 16 kHz mono 16-bit, so the resampling path in `src/speech.py` is not exercised by this data.

## Knowledge base (`data/kb/airport_kb.json`, 32 records)
- Terminal imbalance: 24 records in Terminal 1 vs 8 in Terminal 2; 7 records serve both terminals. Terminal 2 has no `lost_property`, `lounge`, `medical` or `transport` record, so those requests resolve to Terminal 1 records or to a grounded negative.
- Several categories hold a single record (lost_property, lounge, medical), which makes them easy to answer but gives no within-category disambiguation to test.
- The KB is entirely synthetic and hand-authored: hours, directions and contacts are invented, and `volatility` marks the one record (flight information) whose real-world counterpart changes minute by minute.

## Implications for generalisation (dataset-level)
- Results on clean pictograms cannot be assumed to transfer to real airport signage; the photographed subset is small, author-supplied and mostly the same sign family.
- Text and speech results reflect a single author's and a single speaker's phrasing plus TTS; they are evidence of pipeline behaviour, not of coverage of real passenger language.
- The KB's size (32 records) makes exact and alias matching unusually effective; a larger real KB would shift more decisions to the semantic stage, where the thresholds were set on 43 development queries.
